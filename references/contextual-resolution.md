# Lead with the Contextual Resolution Method

The Contextual Resolution Method (CRM), developed by Vontarius Falls, leads every Blueprint engagement. Here CRM names the problem-solving method, not customer relationship management software. A software product, process change, service or adaptation of an existing system can emerge from the inquiry. Keep the user's intended outcome central.

The method moves between the whole problem, its parts, their relationships and the surrounding context. Its six movements are Define → Break down → Look around → Rethink → Create → Check. The first four lead the user's discussion with the agent and determine the research agenda. Create develops the solution and build specification; Check reviews the proposal and later verifies authorized implementation. Return to earlier movements whenever evidence changes the understanding.

## 1. Define the problem

Start with the user's account and existing context. Reflect what happens today, who or what is affected, why it matters and the change they want. Ask a few consequential questions in plain language; a concrete recent example often reveals more than a feature list. Reuse settled answers and designated inventories. If they arrive with a product idea, preserve it as a candidate response while examining the need it serves.

Output: an initial problem brief, known constraints and uncertainties. Keep the initial framing available for comparison with later findings. A supplied observation, a researched finding, an inference, an assumption and an open question retain their distinct statuses; an AI explanation is not established evidence.

## 2. Break the problem down

Map the relevant actors or systems, activities, decisions, inputs, outputs, tools, rules, timing and handoffs. Choose the parts that explain this situation rather than filling every category. Show their relationships: what moves between them, what depends on what, what should happen and where reality differs. A list of components alone is insufficient.

Separate observed symptoms from possible causes. Offer competing explanations where the cause is uncertain and identify what evidence would distinguish them. Let the user correct the map. Avoid converting every symptom into a feature request.

Output: a problem map with meaningful relationships, failure points, assumptions and questions to investigate. Use a legible diagram or an explicit relationship table appropriate to the project. An analysis diagram does not replace the actual UI/UX mockups required later.

## 3. Look around: derive and run the research agenda

Turn the map's consequential unknowns into research questions. For each priority question record:

- The component, relationship or constraint that raised it.
- Which decision or outcome the answer could change.
- How to investigate it: user/domain evidence, existing-system inspection, authoritative sources or a bounded experiment.
- The finding and its evidence, remaining uncertainty, and resulting implication.

Include adjacent workflows, existing solutions, incentives, operating responsibilities, constraints and downstream effects where relevant. Investigate an adjacent area when it could change the problem definition, solution choice, delivery path or evidence of success. Defer branches with no such decision impact and explain why; reopen them if evidence changes. This bounds the inquiry without an arbitrary time or question quota.

Compare reuse, adaptation, a process intervention and new construction as applicable. Tool and infrastructure research must answer the mapped need, rather than defaulting to a familiar stack. Preliminary fact checks may run while the map is still being shaped; feed their findings back into it. Continue useful independent research while awaiting owner answers.

Use research.md for evidence and recommendation standards, and resources.md for the detailed resource/access register. Reuse only designated resources; distinguish confirmed availability, required setup/permission, resources to obtain and unverified assumptions. Newly discovered needs lead to scoped access questions and intended configuration locations. Outstanding access blocks dependent execution, not completion of the design specification.

Output: a prioritized, evidence-linked research agenda with findings, relevant context, resource needs and open decisions.

## 4. Rethink and establish boundaries

Compare the initial framing with the evidence. Explain what changed and why, or why the original understanding still holds. Reconsider causes, boundaries, affected actors, exclusions and the smallest useful intervention. Do not manufacture a changed problem definition or root-cause certainty.

Confirm consequential changes with the user. Distinguish fixed constraints from negotiable ones; define the complete intended outcome, bounded next delivery and observable indications of success. Identify blocking decisions, their resolver and independent work that remains available.

Transition to solution design when the problem/purpose, relevant relationships, decision-driving research, intended outcome and next-delivery boundaries are sufficiently grounded and accepted to choose a response. Reuse existing confirmation rather than requiring a ceremonial approval. An unresolved purpose or scope choice keeps dependent design pending; later setup/access can remain an explicit dependency. Do not wait for every unknown to disappear. For refine/reconcile work, reuse the settled map and revisit only affected areas.

## 5–6. Create and check through Blueprint

Carry the refined problem and evidence into approach selection, resource planning, journeys, visual direction and concept selection, the full mockup set, contracts, critical path and acceptance criteria. Preserve all existing Blueprint visual approval, access and handoff gates. A completed CRM discussion is the foundation for the specification, not completion of the whole Blueprint request; continue available work.

Trace important choices back to the problem: need or relationship → research question/finding → requirement/solution choice → observable check. During design, verify that the proposal addresses the refined problem. Actual implementation verification waits for an implementation request and real evidence. If a mockup, resource constraint, review or test exposes a different problem, reopen the affected map/question and reconcile downstream decisions.

## Preserve the foundation

Keep the initial and refined brief, problem map, evidence distinctions, research questions/findings, boundaries and unresolved decisions in the entry document or a linked PROBLEM.md. Scale to the project; avoid six empty documents or a transcript dump. Link to existing research, decisions and resource records instead of duplicating their current statuses. Include relied-on inquiry documents in the existing handoff snapshot/reconciliation surface when using a visual pack.

All profiles use this method. Default adapts depth to the problem; hackathon prioritizes demo-critical uncertainties and a bounded end-to-end outcome; startup emphasizes user/operating assumptions and evolution. A profile changes emphasis and depth, not the requirement to understand the problem before committing to the solution.
