# Research and collaborative decisions

Use the [Contextual Resolution Method](contextual-resolution.md) to establish the problem map and derive the investigation agenda. Link decision-driving research to its originating component, relationship or contextual uncertainty and the decision it could change. Feed findings back into the map and refined problem definition; research can change the problem, not merely justify a tool.

Inspect existing systems and compare reuse, extension, replacement and greenfield where relevant. Investigate capabilities, costs, integration limits, access/data boundaries, failure modes, operating responsibilities and licensing when they affect feasibility. Use authoritative sources for technical claims and record source/date or revision.

During an initial planning checkpoint, carry out at least the useful independent investigation available now. Missing workflow answers or resource access should narrow research, not postpone all research: inspect relevant existing-solution patterns or authoritative capability documentation while dependent choices remain open. If sources/tools are unavailable, document the concrete limitation.

Before a consequential recommendation, establish evidence, alternatives, tradeoffs and remaining assumptions. Confirm meaningful choices; handle reversible planning details within the user's direction without repeated approvals. Bundle questions that can be answered now and defer those whose prerequisites are unsettled.

Use plain language and concrete consequences. If experience or interaction cannot be resolved conversationally, produce or propose a bounded mockup/experiment and state what it resolves. Separate exploratory artifacts from production implementation; respect tool/spend authorization.

Research is sufficient for a decision when viable options and material consequences are understood and remaining assumptions are visible. If inspection/research is unavailable, mark feasibility provisional and continue unaffected design. Do not invent citations, user evidence, capabilities or measured results.

Persist essential findings, evidence, chosen direction, meaningful rejected alternatives and unresolved questions. Avoid transcript dumps. The conversation evolves naturally while the blueprint records its current meaning.
